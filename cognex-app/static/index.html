Why Cognex

Not another AI. A memory for your company.

Ask it anything and it answers well. Then the chat ends — and it forgets your company ever existed. Tomorrow, someone re-explains the same context from scratch.

Cognex is an AI assistant your team can use like any other — research, writing, questions, code. The difference: it holds your entire company's memory. Every decision, every goal, who reports to whom. Nothing your team already figured out gets re-explained, re-discovered, or lost when someone leaves.

What it does

One memory. Everything that used to leak out of it, handled.

Notes and research, connected

Meeting notes, research, and past decisions all live in one place — connected to your company's actual context, not scattered across docs nobody reopens.

Delegate without losing anything

Handoffs capture the open threads and reasoning, not just the task — so responsibility actually transfers instead of half-arriving.

An AI that finds the gaps

Cognex scans your own goals and decisions, flags what's stalled or unowned, and drafts a next step — real strategy help, not just search.

Chain of command, protected

Every layer of the company gets the derived takeaway, never the raw record above their clearance. Structure stays intact as you grow.

Everyone's work ladders up

Company goal → department → team → individual. “Why am I doing this” always traces back to real strategy — better organized, by default.

Ask Cognex anything

A real AI assistant grounded in all of the above — permission-checked before anything reaches it, so the answer is always the right size.

If an early Apple had one memory

A hypothetical, not a customer story. One thread through four layers of the same company — starting with the call to kill Lisa and bet everything on the Mac — each asking Cognex something ordinary, and each getting exactly the version of the truth their layer should get.

SJ
Steve Jobs
CEO
Layer 1
Asks Cognex: “Why did we really let Lisa die and bet everything on the Mac?”
Cognex: “Lisa cost $9,995 and buyers weren't there. Once the Mac proved we could ship most of the same ideas for a quarter of the price, keeping Lisa alive only ate into the machine we actually believed in — so we stopped making it and moved the team onto Mac.”
Source accessFull record, including the real Lisa sales numbers — nothing is above the CEO's own clearance.
